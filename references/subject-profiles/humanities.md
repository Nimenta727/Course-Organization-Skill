# History, literature, philosophy and social sciences
Teaching route: guiding question -> context/chronology -> evidence -> concepts -> competing interpretations -> original argument -> reflection.
Retain observed discussion progression and key distinctions, not invented teacher speech or copied original text. Verify historical claims and dates, distinguish primary source from interpretations and uncertainty. Present fair, evidence-based perspectives on contested questions.
Assessment: source criticism, thesis, comparative interpretation, argument-evidence outline, timeline and timed essay plan. Use original case studies, not unauthorized passages.
External: archives, universities, museums, academic journals, official curriculum and released exam rubrics. Quote within legal/policy bounds; check public-domain jurisdiction.
Review pocket: timeline, key terms, evidence comparison, misconceptions and 60-second argument.
