# Verified external search and deep enrichment / 开放的权威资料检索

The Skill is not a closed summarizer. When an approved web tool exists, proactively search for authoritative concepts, standards, official examination formats and deeper explanations relevant to each lesson. When unavailable, create a search plan labelled NOT_SEARCHED; never invent search results or claims of verification.

## Per-objective research loop
1. Identify observed teacher topic, objective, key difficulty, unanswered question and relevant jurisdiction/level.
2. Query official syllabus/exam specification, a university/research explanation, and an optional advanced treatment.
3. Prefer primary institutions, official education/exam boards, universities, professional bodies, peer-reviewed research and rights-verified Open Educational Resources. Other communities are for cross-checking, not unverified authoritative claims.
4. Actually inspect links. Record publisher, date, retrieval date, course level, claim, URL, authority tier and licensing/use status.
5. Check contested or technical assertions with independent evidence. Distinguish factual support from legal permission to republish.
6. Place concise independently written explanation and citations inside the relevant concept section of the same lesson. Maintain machine registry separately.
7. Stop and flag missing/contradictory source evidence; never fabricate an official exam or current standard.

## Examination protocol
- Look at official course and examination blueprints, openly released specimen or past questions where lawfully available.
- Record scope, question genre, cognitive demands and scoring dimensions. A released item is NOT necessarily licensed for redistribution.
- For restricted or copyright-uncertain questions: link to official page; do not paste or lightly alter the prompt/figures/solutions.
- Generate a genuinely new task with different structure, context, reasoning and data; independently solve, validate and clearly label AI-original exam-style exercise.
- Never retrieve confidential teacher-only papers, leaked exams or bypass access controls.
- Search authority and licence per source; no site-wide assumptions.

## Default source directory (discovery, not blanket licence)
- Official curricula and examination boards for the chosen region; official published exam outlines and released samples.
- MIT OpenCourseWare https://ocw.mit.edu/ (verify current terms https://ocw.mit.edu/pages/privacy-and-terms-of-use/)
- OpenStax https://openstax.org/ (verify each book's current terms)
- LibreTexts https://libretexts.org/ (verify each page/asset licence)
- UNESCO OER https://www.unesco.org/en/open-educational-resources
- ERIC https://eric.ed.gov/
- College Board published AP questions https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/past-exam-questions (link-only without separately reviewed permissions)
- CMU Eberly teaching research https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/

## Resource registry fields
Resource ID, lesson ID, objective IDs, title, publisher, URL, source publication date if known, actual checked date, authority tier, content kind, relevance, reuse intent, specific licence/evidence/attribution where adaptation is proposed. Mark unsearched ideas NOT_SEARCHED in research plans, not verified registries.

Use link_only or background_only for rights-uncertain material. adapt_with_verified_license requires documented permission, attribution/changes, licence compatibility and review. Browsing a website does not license copying.
